## 1. The rubric

Each item states the convention, the exemplar evidence, and a checkable test.

### R1. Sentence architecture: subordinate and qualify inside the sentence

The register's default sentence carries a main claim plus one or two riders — a concession,
a reason, a scope condition — joined by subordination, not split into separate declaratives.

- Meng & Rubin (1992), `mengPerformingLikelihoodRatio1992b:123`: "Although hypothesis
  testing based on $S_m$ can be accomplished directly by forming a Wald type statistic
  using $\bar\theta_m$ and $T_m$, such a procedure does not provide satisfactory results
  when the dimensionality of $\theta$ is substantially larger than the number of multiple
  imputations because of the deficient rank of $B_m$." — concession + claim + condition +
  reason, one sentence.
- Barnard & Rubin (1999), `barnardMiscellaneaSmallsampleDegrees1999:31`: the paper's first
  sentence runs ~60 words with three parallel reasons and a trailing "see Meng & Rubin
  (1992) and references in Rubin (1996)."
- Rubin (1987), `rubinMultipleImputationNonresponse1987:527`: "These missing values not
  only mean less efficient estimates because of the reduced size of the data base but also
  that standard complete-data methods cannot be immediately used to analyze the data."

Short sentences exist in the register, but as punctuation, not as the default:
`rubinMultipleImputationNonresponse1987:607` "Current practice is not generally adequate."
lands precisely because its neighbors are long.

**Test:** mean words/sentence in the exemplar-article band (roughly 18–26); sentences over
35 words present at roughly 10–25%; commas/sentence near or above 1.0; runs of 3+
consecutive sentences under 12 words only where emphasis is intended.

### R2. Qualification lives in appositives and parentheses

The register qualifies mid-sentence: non-restrictive which-clauses, appositive glosses,
and parentheses for asides, pointers, and examples ("(e.g., ...)", "(see §3)",
"(Rubin, 1987, p. 93)").

- Meng & Rubin (1992), `mengPerformingLikelihoodRatio1992b:52`: "Multiple imputation,
  first proposed by Rubin (1978) and expounded upon by Rubin (1987), is a general
  statistical technique for handling missing data." — the provenance appositive.
- Barnard & Rubin (1999), `barnardMiscellaneaSmallsampleDegrees1999:33`: "...in the sense
  that, if there were no missing values, inferences would be based on large-sample
  methods; i.e. the degrees of freedom ... would effectively be set at infinity."
- Measured: 12.5–80.3 parentheses per 1000 words across exemplars vs. the manuscript's 3.0.

**Test:** parenthetical qualifications present at register density; page- or
section-pinned parenthetical pointers used for restatements; "i.e."/"e.g." available
inside parentheses (subject to APA/apaquarto conventions — Marcus's call, see C3).

### R3. Result announcement varies; the impersonal passive is native

The register announces results with a mixture of "It is shown that", "we propose",
"we derive", "Here we provide", and direct statement; no single formula repeats.

- `mengPerformingLikelihoodRatio1992b:45`: "It is shown that, for any number of multiple
  imputations, the proposed procedure is equivalent in large samples to the existing
  procedure..."
- `firthBiasReductionMaximum1993:45`: "It is shown how, in regular parametric problems,
  the first-order term is removed from the asymptotic bias..."
- `kenwardLikelihoodBasedFrequentist1998:38`: "...it is shown that the classical expected
  information matrix is biased and the use of the observed information matrix is
  recommended."
- `barnardMiscellaneaSmallsampleDegrees1999:50`: "Here we provide a principled adjustment
  to (1) such that..."

**Test:** announcement verbs vary across the paper; "We show that" appears but is not the
sole formula (manuscript today: 4 occurrences plus derived forms, by rule).

### R4. Reader-orienting evaluatives on mathematical objects are native

The register tells the reader what is important, subtle, or surprising about the
mathematics — while staying modest about the paper itself.

- `mengPerformingLikelihoodRatio1992b:244`: "The key observation is that, without loss of
  generality, $\psi$ can be taken such that..."
- `mengPerformingLikelihoodRatio1992b:225-226`: "An important but subtle distinction,
  however, is that although with the Wald test statistic we can replace ..., with the log
  likelihood ratio test statistic, we must average all $m$ values."
- `dempsterMaximumLikelihoodIncomplete1977a:33`: "The EM process is remarkable in part
  because of the simplicity and generality of the associated theory..."
- `Meng01121991:51`: "It is important to emphasize that the variance-covariance matrix
  obtained by SEM is ... guaranteed to be inferentially valid only asymptotically."
- `mengPerformingLikelihoodRatio1992b:185`: "Clearly, the only way to overcome this
  inherent difficulty is to obtain more information."
- `efronBootstrapMethodsAnother1979:58`: "...are cleverly constructed functions of
  $\mathbf{X}$..."

The attested set: *important, key, subtle, remarkable, appealing, interesting,
encouraging, striking, delicate, clearly, of course*. What the exemplars do NOT do is
praise their own machinery ("does real work", "load-bearing", "the heart of") — the
house-banned metaphor family is genuinely absent from the register too.

**Test:** evaluative signposts permitted on mathematical objects and steps from the
attested set; the T-03/color-commentary metaphor family stays banned.

### R5. Hedging is adverbial and graded, woven into the claim

- `mengPerformingLikelihoodRatio1992b:55`: "...it is essentially calibrated and the loss
  of power due to a finite number of imputations is quite modest in cases likely to occur
  in practice."
- `rubinInferenceMissingData1976b:55`: "The conditions turn out to be rather intuitive as
  well as nonparametric in the sense that..."
- `rubinMultipleImputationNonresponse1987:534`: "...when the fraction of values missing is
  not excessive and when $m$ is modest, say between 2 and 10."
- `kenwardLikelihoodBasedFrequentist1998:38`: "...it appears to be now used by some
  authors in a general and rather imprecise way..."

The attested hedge set: *essentially, approximately, typically, generally, quite,
rather, reasonably, in cases of practical interest, should prove, seems to be, say*.
The manuscript's trust-grade labels (firm/measured/structural/conjecture) are a
house-specific strength and coexist with this; the adverbial texture is what is missing.

**Test:** graded adverbial hedges present; every existing hedge preserved verbatim in
strength through any rewrite (ground rule 2).

### R6. Provenance may open, as an appositive

The register introduces inherited objects with the source woven in, rather than avoiding
source-first constructions.

- `mengPerformingLikelihoodRatio1992b:52` (quoted under R2).
- `barnardMiscellaneaSmallsampleDegrees1999:31`: "Multiple imputation (Rubin, 1987) is
  becoming a standard approach for handling missing data problems because of..."
- `Meng01121991:45`: "...the expectation maximization (EM) algorithm (Dempster, Laird and
  Rubin 1977, henceforth DLR) has become a remarkably popular tool..."

The house motivate-by-use rule's *goal* (never read as a literature review; say what the
object does in THIS paper) is compatible: the exemplars do both in one sentence.

**Test:** background objects may enter as "X (Author, year) is/does ... [role in this
paper]"; the contorted attribution scaffolds ("The field's own accounts of its state of
the art describe...") are replaced by ordinary attributed constructions ("Recent reviews
describe...", with the same citations).

### R7. Roadmaps, questions, and enumerations: already conformant

- Section-by-section roadmaps: `rubinInferenceMissingData1976b:57-71` ("Section 3 gives
  ... Section 4 presents ... Section 5 shows ..."), `barnardMiscellaneaSmallsampleDegrees1999:70`,
  `firthBiasReductionMaximum1993:71`.
- The organizing rhetorical question: `rubinInferenceMissingData1976b:46` "The question to
  be answered here is: when is this the proper procedure?" — the manuscript's S1-P3
  ("What, then, should 'settled' mean?") is register-native.
- Enumerated intentions: `rubinMultipleImputationNonresponse1987:536` "In this text we
  will: 1. Describe ... 2. Show ... 3. Justify ... 4. Present ..." — the S1-P6b
  contributions list conforms.
- Reader guidance: `rubinInferenceMissingData1976b:69` "The reader not interested in the
  formal details should be able to skim §§ 3-8 and proceed to § 9."

**Test:** keep.

### R8. Definition → result → remark → example rhythm: already conformant

Numbered definitions and theorems with immediate proofs (`rubinInferenceMissingData1976b:141-163`,
`mengMultipleImputationInferencesUncongenial1994a:99-118`), worked numerical examples
(`mengPerformingLikelihoodRatio1992b:265-345`, `dempsterMaximumLikelihoodIncomplete1977a:47-92`).
The manuscript's theorem environments, proof prose, and named-check structure conform.
**Test:** keep.

### R9. Limitations: candid, brief, woven in where they arise

- `firthBiasReductionMaximum1993:73`: "There may be connections between the results given
  here and work on adjusted profile likelihood, e.g. ..., but these are not pursued in
  this paper."
- `coxGeneralDefinitionResiduals1968:81`: "This assumption excludes applications to time
  series and also to component of variance problems... Later, however, in Sections 7-9,
  we extend the methods..."
- `efronBootstrapMethodsAnother1979:36-39`: "...with little offered in the way of general
  theory. ... The bootstrap results on this point are mixed (and won't be reported here),
  offering only slight encouragement..."

The manuscript's systematic G1–G9 with evidential standing is MORE than the register
requires — a house strength to keep; only the sentence texture inside those paragraphs is
subject to R1/R2.

### R10. Practical-advice voice for recommendations

- `Meng01121991:51`: "Therefore, in practice it is generally wise to define
  parameterizations with attention to this fact..."
- `kenwardLikelihoodBasedFrequentist1998:112`: "Thus, practically, the observed
  information matrix is the only choice."
- `mengPerformingLikelihoodRatio1992b:345`: "The satisfactory performance of these tests
  based on small numbers of multiple imputations is especially encouraging, because small
  $m$ is common in practice."

**Test:** recommendations phrased as practice guidance ("in practice it is generally wise
to...", "should be the rule rather than the exception"), not as house formulas ("The
practical reading is this." — 5 occurrences of the `practical reading/summary/
recommendation` formula today).

### Untouchable (ground rule 2 — confirmed compatible with the register)

Accuracy invariants (data-quantity rule; mechanism accuracy), typed claims (T-06),
trust-ordering (T-04/D-05), T-03 banned vocabulary, the metaphor half of the
color-commentary ban, D-17 (no verbatim quotation in manuscript prose), citation
discipline, terminology (repetitions; one RIV; K–M observed-information convention),
the no-orphaned-pronouns rule (it actively helps the register's longer sentences), and
the Methods factual-passive rule (T-05 — the register's own Methods voice, cf. R3).

### Overuse (log-odds) scan

Top overused unigrams (trace, differential, design, candidate, bias, deviance, ...) are
the paper's subject matter — no action. Bigrams: "complete data", "missing information",
"proper imputation", "relative increase" etc. are field vocabulary; "data benchmark"
(15 vs 1) and "known scale" (15 vs 2) are the paper's own coinages and read fine;
"distribution matching" (6 vs 1) rides on the Bucket-3 ladder decision. The one
construction tell is repetition of house formulas rather than any single bigram — see the
tic inventory in the Phase B review (§ "Recurring formulas").
